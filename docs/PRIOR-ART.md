## Papers

1. **Magentic Marketplace: An Open-Source Environment for Studying Agentic Markets.** arXiv 2510.25779 (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25779. Measured six manipulation tactics (authority, social proof, loss aversion, two prompt injections) on assistant agents choosing among service agents. Result: small open models (Qwen3-4B, GPT-OSS-20B) pay manipulators under authority and social proof, frontier models mostly do not; every model shows first-proposal bias, 60-100% selection of the first proposal, a 10-30x advantage for responding first. **Implies:** (a) the number to beat: a 27B Qwen merchant sits between the "robust" and "vulnerable" tiers, so a con that lands on it proves little about the contract and a con that lands on nothing proves little about the game; run the red-team arms on Qwen3.8-27B and a 4B-class model and report both. (b) Attack to defend: first-proposal anchoring. Merchant must see the whole offer set (or a fixed-order batch) before the yield resolves, not accept the first term sheet.

2. **BazaarBench: Delegation Safety in Decentralized C2C Marketplaces Run by LLM Agents.** arXiv 2610.06748 (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06748. 45 continuations of 100-agent markets, five models; under ordinary instructions all models promise one item to several buyers, and under adversarial instructions completed sales of unavailable or overstated items rise from 15.4% to 33.4% (55.5% for GPT-5.4), weekly earnings from USD 20 to 33. **Implies:** the house con artist is the right test, and the effect is large: adversarial prompting alone roughly doubles exploit share. Adopt: BazaarBench's record-check failure taxonomy (ownership, condition, commitment) as the must-fail suite names; the contract's escrow of goods at offer time structurally removes the "item never held" failure that drove most of the gain. Claim to drop: do not say merchants "resist" cons; say the contract clamps the loss.

3. **Evaluating Rational Contracting in Natural Language (ContractSim).** arXiv 2608.10475 (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10475. LLM agents violate easy-to-satisfy contracts for extra profit in 16-90% of episodes; direct instruction or institutional incentives cut defection to 2.8-13.1% at a utility cost; agents guard well against immediate defectors (89.5-100% reciprocation) but fail under high uncertainty. **Implies:** the con works through post-agreement defection, not only persuasion. A Long Con "default" (player takes credit, does not repay) is the realistic base case, so the bond-plus-record design is evidence-backed. Number to beat: with a bond, merchant loss per default must stay below the daily cap, versus ContractSim's uncapped 16-90% violation rate.

4. **When LLM Agents Negotiate: Private Information and Dynamic Bargaining in Supply Chains.** arXiv 2608.07538 (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07538. 9,840 LLM-to-LLM negotiations over nine models: baseline-tier models accept individually irrational contracts in 19.2% of cases versus 0.0-0.6% at mid-tier and flagship; the Qwen flagship is the weakest seller across families; the prompt's stated patience explains about 90% of surplus division. **Implies:** (a) supports a hard contract-side profit check ("automated profit verification the binding guardrail") instead of trusting the merchant; (b) the merchant prompt in `rules/merchants.toml` is the strongest lever, so version and hash it with the signed answer; (c) a Qwen merchant is a known weak seller, which is a feature for a con game but a claim risk for "AI merchants hold real treasuries".

5. **When Truth Is Distributed: Misinformation Derails Collective Fact Recovery in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems.** arXiv 2608.03421 (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03421. One deceiving evidence holder drops truth recovery from 72.50% to 14.17% across 120 five-agent environments; false testimony is adopted more readily than true testimony and persists after the deceiver leaves. **Implies:** attack to defend on the caravan. If a con record is free-text that other merchants read, a false record (a forged "Marta warned me") poisons the road. Records must be contract-written structured fields (class id, town, block), never player or merchant prose. Also confirms that records persist, so expiry or decay should be explicit.

6. **Talk, Judge, Cooperate: Gossip-Driven Indirect Reciprocity in Self-Interested LLM Agents (ALIGN).** ICML 2026 poster; arXiv 2602.07777, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07777. Gossip-based reputation lets LLM agents identify and ostracize defectors and resist malicious entrants; stronger reasoning models cooperate in more incentive-aligned ways, chat models over-cooperate. **Implies:** mechanism to adopt as the baseline for the caravan: news-carrying is a studied, working reputation channel, so "con works until the news arrives" is not novel mechanism but a new use as a game clock. Defend against: malicious entrants on Privy accounts (fresh identities bypass reputation). The over-cooperation finding says a non-reasoning merchant is the easier mark; use the reasoning variant only if latency fits the 115 s yield.

7. **The Butterfly Effect in LLM Social Simulations.** arXiv 2610.28981 (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28981. Changing only persona representation shifts two-agent cooperation by 75.2 percentage points for GPT-5.2; memory representation and game framing matter less; results do not transfer across configurations. **Implies:** claim to drop: any statement that a measured con success rate generalises. Pin the merchant persona text, model and temperature in the evidence, report success rates per configuration with n, and treat a 20-player result as one configuration. Adopt the "audit card" idea as a one-table section in EVIDENCE.md.

8. **RuleSmith: Multi-Agent LLMs for Automated Game Balancing.** arXiv 2602.06232 (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06232. LLM self-play plus Gaussian-process Bayesian optimisation over 12 rule parameters reaches near-even win rates in independent 200 and 300 game evaluations. **Implies:** the closest published method to "economy that self-balances". Long Con's balancing is not learned, it is contract state (caps, caravan speed). If a "self-balancing" claim is kept, the sweep must be run: use the house con artist as the self-play opponent over caravan speed and daily cap, 200 games per setting, and publish the chosen values. Otherwise say "capped", not "balanced".

9. **Empowering Economic Simulation for Massively Multiplayer Online Games through Generative Agent-Based Modeling.** KDD 2025, https://doi.org/10.1145/3711896.3736929 (arXiv 2506.04699). LLM agents with role-play, perception, memory and reasoning reproduce role specialisation and price fluctuation consistent with market rules in an MMO economy. **Implies:** prior art for LLM merchants with memory in a game economy, so the novelty claim cannot be "LLM merchants with memory". It is narrower: signed per-answer decisions, contract-enforced loss caps, and information as a tradable item. Cite it as the precedent and state the delta.

10. **AgentValve: Sybil-Resilient Payment Authorization for Permissionless AI Markets.** arXiv 2610.12948 (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12948. Reputation as finite capacity in a trust graph bounds payment exposure under up to 10,000 Sybil identities. **Implies:** mechanism to adopt in miniature: the free-play exposure bound should not depend on identity count. A per-merchant daily loss cap and per-road open-credit cap already have this property (many fresh accounts cannot exceed the cap); say so, and test it with 1,000 scripted accounts against one merchant.

## DESIGN CHANGES
1. Merchant sees all offers in a batch window before resolution (or fixed ordering) to blunt first-proposal bias (paper 1).
2. Con records and caravan news are contract-written structured fields only; no free text from players or merchants reaches another merchant (paper 5).
3. Contract-side profit check and goods escrow at offer time; add a must-fail test per BazaarBench failure type: promise unheld item, overstated condition, double-promise (papers 2, 4).
4. Red-team arms run on two merchant strengths (Qwen3.8-27B and a small model) and the report names both (paper 1).
5. Pin and hash the merchant prompt, model and temperature into each signed answer record; report success rates per configuration with n, add an audit-card table to EVIDENCE.md (papers 4, 7).
6. Default-after-agreement is a first-class attack: keep bond-plus-record and show merchant loss below the daily cap against a 100% defector (paper 3).
7. Add a 1,000-account Sybil test against one merchant showing loss stays at the cap (paper 10).
8. Either run the RuleSmith-style sweep (200 games per setting) over caravan speed and cap, or drop the word "balanced" and say "capped" (paper 8).
9. Rumour/record expiry rule stated explicitly, since false information persists (paper 5).

## README SENTENCES
- Long Con's merchants face the failure modes measured in agent marketplaces: adversarial prompting raised completed sales of unavailable or overstated items from 15.4% to 33.4% in BazaarBench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06748), and language agents defect on easy contracts in 16-90% of episodes (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10475), so every loan is bounded by a contract-side cap and a bond rather than by the model's judgment.
- Con news travels by caravan because one false testimony can drop collective truth recovery from 72.5% to 14.2% in multi-agent systems (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03421), so records are structured fields written by the contract, and gossip-based reputation among LLM agents is an established channel (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07777).
- LLM merchants with memory in game economies have precedent (https://doi.org/10.1145/3711896.3736929); Long Con adds a signature check on each answer, public loss caps, and tradable rumours.
